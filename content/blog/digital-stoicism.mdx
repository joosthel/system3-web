If you follow AI news, you know how loud it gets. Hype, drama, buzzwords. Every model release gets framed as the one that changes everything, and underneath it all runs the same message: your job is about to disappear.

It's easy to get pulled into that loop. I notice it in myself. The mood swings fast between losing hope and feeling like I have to seize this exact moment or miss it.

After stepping away from the timeline for a while, I've landed on a simpler view. The drama isn't the whole story. AI will shift how we work and live, no question. But those shifts are messier and slower than the headlines suggest. I see it in my own projects: the technology needs real work around it before it creates any value. Oversight, testing, patient iteration.

Not everyone has a personal AI assistant yet. Not everyone needs one. The advances are exciting and they have serious implications. They're still not worth losing sleep over.

There's a lot of talk about agents handling payments, self-reinforcing AI systems, companies run end to end by machines. Some of that will happen in some form. We're also just not there yet. And even in a more automated future, you'll still walk to the bakery and buy bread from someone who knows your name. The world doesn't change overnight. People adapt.

So a stoic approach makes sense to me. Focus on what you can control, accept what you can't. Testing new tools and playing with the latest video models is a fun part of my job. But what counts in the end is whether the work is good and whether it's useful to the people it's made for. Flooding the world with low-effort content helps no one.

And humans still want human connection. We're social creatures by design. That's not changing anytime soon.

So: go outside. Have dinner with your friends. The technology will still be here when you get back.
